South Downs Way Backpacking

The trip started with an evening train to Lewes arriving in around 7:30. It was strange to think a few hours prior there was me sat keyboard bashing behind a screen. The evening sun was starting to get good and really helping the barley fields glow which would be a key feature of the whole trip. There was 2 hours to try and get nicely on route and into an area suitably remote to camp - something identified as a challenge based on the landscape being very open with few good hiding places.

Image

Leaving Lewes, I got onto the South Downs Way near Ilford. It was a beautiful route getting out into ‘proper’ countryside relatively quickly. One big steep slope onto the ridge and that was most of the exertion for the evening. Once on top it was a gentle cruise with the odd finger pointing excitement when a train went past or a little skylark joined for a sing song.

After 9 on the Friday it was getting properly dark and with field options depleting the decision was made to camp along a hedge row in a newly harvested field. While not exactly discrete in location, the only people encountered since leaving Lewes were two lovely teens an hour previous zooming on electric motorbikes with strawberry vapes - it seemed like there would be nobody to offend. Camp pitch was horrendous by positioning the tent so the door managed to go the other side to see the view. No.1 guide of camping is also to not camp over stones but at 2am mid painful sleep it came out that my bones were being slowly eroded by intense rock to body contact.

Waking up properly was surprisingly easy, 5am Garmin alarm and 5:15am pack down. Having finished off breakfast flapjack (sadly no picture to be found) a farmer appeared with his defender and two dogs. Luckily all that was visible was a happy flapjack enjoyer stood slightly too far from the path. The discrete camp was cutting it very fine at the end but ultimately successful.

Properly getting a move on at 5:45, Southease was quickly arrived at, taking a moment to look at the church with its round spire. Mentally noting the station for future trips, it was then up Itford Hill before a nice long easy saunter along the ridge into Alfriston. During this saunter there are numerous tumuli and long barrows which add a few nice little pockets of wonder and present the obvious hope of being a little boy who stumbles upon some long lost treasure. Sadly nothing was found other than a curiously large group of paragliders who seemed to be having an all-round good time with the exception of one chap who was doing some form of experimental dance on the ground with his kite. Deep in my pack was my cameras and I couldn’t be bothered to stop and regurgitate all my possessions to take pics of any of this so everything here is straight from iPhone annoyingly.
