I am a cultural geographer with a particular interest in socio-ecological relations, visual methods, and ethnographies.

Illuminating the Shadows: The Illustrative Photographer's Gaze Upon Post-War Social Housing

Photographs shape our geographical imagination. Previously, they have been used to present the architecture of post-war social housing as being a causal factor in creating neighbourhoods of crime and antisocial behaviour. However, this representation is increasingly being challenged by hobbyist photographers who are seeking to depict the estates in a positive light. Such aestheticised imagery is also critiqued, but for its supposed disregard for political histories and social life.

As part of a general stance whereby amateur photography is considered trivial, no studies have paid attention to the photographers producing such work. Hence, the individuals behind the lens are completely shadowed. This study focuses on them, using unique methods to reveal the political space in which they already exist. Ultimately, it provides evidence that photography is a highly valuable tool to develop ethical knowledge at the personal level.

Participants are shown to be vocally aware of potential representational issues, yet value is still attributed to their work's ability to enforce particular counter-narratives. Finally, the personal nature of all the projects is revealed as making any political responsibilities a sensitive topic.
